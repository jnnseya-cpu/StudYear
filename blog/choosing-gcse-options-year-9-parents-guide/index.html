Choosing GCSE options: a Year 9 parent's guide

3 min read · Published 2026-08-01 · Updated 2026-08-03

Choosing GCSE options is the first big fork in a child's education — and most families make it with too little information. Here is how to decide with confidence.

Start with strengths, not subjects

Better grades come from subjects a child is genuinely good at and enjoys. Look at real evidence — recent assessment scores — rather than what friends are picking.

Keep doors open

The English Baccalaureate core (English, maths, science, a language and a humanity) keeps the widest range of post-16 routes available. For the official rules, see GOV.UK on the EBacc.

Match choices to where they might go

From 2028 the government is putting technical routes on an equal footing with academic ones, so a child can combine strong GCSEs with a clear line into an apprenticeship, T-Level or university.

FAQ

What GCSEs keep the most options open?

The EBacc core — English, maths, the sciences, a language and a humanities subject — keeps the widest set of A-level, apprenticeship and university routes available.

Should my child pick subjects they enjoy or subjects that look impressive?

Enjoyment and strength usually win: children work harder and score higher in subjects they like, and good grades open more doors than an impressive-looking but poorly-graded subject.
